# MACE video: capture, playback and framelock

The O2 I/O chip, MACE, carries the machine's video input and output. It has two input channels and one output channel, each with its own DMA engine, and it sits beside CRIME and GBE: CRIME routes the three video interrupts, GBE takes a framelock signal from MACE and can send its own picture back to memory for MACE to play out. The video kernel driver `mvp.o` reaches the three channels, and the GBE capture block, through pointers its controller record holds ([Driver access](#driver-access)); the X module, `Xsgi` and the `dmedia` video libraries reach none of them directly (they use ioctls). The register chapters below rest on the MACE specification and the public drivers, and the driver chapter on `mvp.o` read in 6.5.30.

Labels follow [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md): **established** is read in full from the cited page, **inferred** is a reasoned reading, **unknown** is not settled by any source in hand. Source key: **[mace]** is the *MACE I/O ASIC Specification*, chip revision 2.0, 31 May 1996, the video chapter dated 15 July 1996 ([docs/sources/specs/mace-2.0-specification.pdf](sources/specs/mace-2.0-specification.pdf), SHA-256 `ecbb68807837ee71dffe6296331f6477f7b0990a4d459f9ec4e6e0d15cd748aa`). Its page numbers equal the PDF page numbers. **[spec]** is the CRIME 1.5 specification and **[gbe-spec]** the GBE specification, as in the other chapters. The video chapter calls the board by its development name and calls the digital camera "moosecam"; the shipped camera is the O2Cam.

Scope: the spec is a design document of 1996. It lists seven unsettled design questions of its own (page 12): VITC and closed-caption data, detecting loss of sync, detecting loss of video clock, changing filter parameters on a field boundary, pause and resume of a stream, the older capture-board pixel order, and the minimum input field width for mipmaps. Established as open in the source; nothing in hand answers them.

## Windows and interrupts

- Established ([mace] PDF 32, table 4): the video registers sit in three 0x80000-byte sets, at offsets `0x100000` (input channel 1), `0x180000` (input channel 2) and `0x200000` (output channel) from the MACE register base. Inferred: the base is `0x1f000000`, which puts the sets at `0x1f100000`, `0x1f180000` and `0x1f200000` which are the addresses the inventory configuration takes from the Linux `struct sgi_mace` layout. Every register is 64-bit aligned and takes 64-bit accesses; undefined bits read as zero ([mace] PDF 32).
- Established ([spec] PDF 107-108, table 5-5): CRIME interrupt input 0 is video input channel 1, input 1 is video input channel 2 and input 2 is the video output channel, each with the R10000 interrupt number 3 in the table's last column (inferred reading of that column). This agrees with the interrupt numbering of [netbsd] noted in [memory.md](memory.md) and fixes the three video inputs among the sixteen MACE-routed ones.
- Established ([spec] PDF 109, section 5.7.2): bit 31 of the CRIME interrupt status register is a registered copy of the VICE interrupt pin, read-only. That is a second source for `CRIME_INT_VICE` in [devices/crime/CRIME.h](../devices/crime/CRIME.h), which cited [netbsd] alone. See [vice.md](vice.md).

## Ports and routing

- Established ([mace] PDF 13-17): four video sources feed two chip input ports. Source A is the analog decoder, B the primary general-purpose D1 port, C the digital camera and D the secondary general-purpose D1 port. Sources are multiplexed outside the chip, so the chip sees two ports, AB and CD. The analog inputs are selected over the I2C bus and the AB/CD choice by the hardware configuration register `VHW_CFG`.
- Established ([mace] PDF 16-17): the output streams can be fed back into the input path, so either input channel can take port AB, port CD, output port E or output port F. Both channels may select the same port, which lets software capture full size while showing a reduced copy. Two channels may also carry YUV and alpha separately, but the alpha is never merged by hardware; software merges the two buffers.
- Established ([mace] PDF 17): one output channel feeds two chip output ports. Port E also drives the analog encoder and port F is a general-purpose D1 port. Each port can send the YUV stream, the alpha stream, or the genlock input passed straight through. Both ports run the same timing and both emit 10-bit D1 with embedded control codes, with separate blanking and field signals on port E for the encoder.
- Established ([mace] PDF 18): output timing is either free-running from a fixed 27 MHz clock (a 13.5 MHz pixel clock) or genlocked to a D1 input port or an external horizontal, vertical and field sync source. The output can run no faster than 30 MHz. The programmed timing must match the genlock source; any remaining offset is the programmer's, through the pad registers.
- Established ([mace] PDF 19-20, 53-55): two sync signals leave the chip. One goes to the audio codec at the horizontal rate of a chosen D1 stream, and the second always follows the video output. A framelock signal at the vertical rate goes to GBE, selected from output ports E/F, either input port or the external sync.

- Inferred (secondhand, [notes on O2 video input](sources/reports/lurkertech-mvp-video-input-notes.html), SHA-256 in [sources/README.md](sources/README.md)): the IRIX video library module calls this hardware "mvp", short for MACE video ports. It lists the camera, composite and S-video jacks, a digital-video dongle on the camera connector, the primary and secondary D1 inputs, and two loopback inputs (the output stream, and its alpha stream) as video sources. The loopback pair matches input sources "output port E" and "output port F" of `VIN.CONFIG` above, and the digital camera connector matches source C. The note says the two general-purpose D1 inputs exist only on an extended audio-video board that was never sold, which explains why the shipped machine has no serial D1 connectors although the chip routes them. The note also says the hardware detects the timing of an incoming signal and converts between square and non-square pixels, both of which the specification describes (the latter on PDF 13-14), and gives linear or tiled buffers.

### GBE framelock and capture, from the GBE side

- Established ([gbe-spec] PDF 19-20, section 2.9): GBE has no interlaced timing; interlaced output is the MACE output channel's job. GBE's framelock input pin is driven by MACE from an external video source, and it presets the vertical counter to the value in the frame-lock timing register. This locks frame period only, not individual pixels ("this is not genlock"). To keep the picture from jumping when the two rates differ slightly, the preset line should sit early in vertical blanking. The framelock input is synchronised to the dot clock.
- Established ([gbe-spec] PDF 23, section 2.12): GBE can write its 140 MHz pixel stream back to main memory, filtered down to NTSC or PAL data rates, as 768 by 288 for interlaced square-pixel PAL or 640 by 240 for NTSC (or unfiltered at 1:1). GBE always produces square pixels; the square-to-non-square conversion happens in MACE on the way out. That is the producer end of the loopback documented here and is a second source for the filter ratios below.
- Unknown: how the shipped IRIX video software programs `VHW_CFG` for framelock or what the `rrmGenlockNotify` hooks of [functions.md](functions.md) read. Routes tried: [mace], [gbe-spec], [spec], the kernel objects and the X module (no access to the MACE video window found), `mvp.o` (the three routines that write `VHW_CFG` are read, in [The writers of VHW_CFG](#the-writers-of-vhw_cfg); they take the framelock fields from the device settings and the input index, and do not name a framelock mode). Next: `mvp.so`.

## Filtering and conversion

- Established ([mace] PDF 13-14, 21-22): the input path applies, in this order and all optionally, a non-square to square filter, a horizontal down-scale, a vertical down-scale and mipmap generation; all work in YUV with subsampled chroma. The non-square filter is a Mitchell filter with fixed ratios: NTSC 720 to 654 (10/11), PAL 720 to 786 (12/11), or half width. The arbitrary down-scale is a block filter that averages weighted input pixels; scaling by one half uses weights of a quarter, a half and a quarter. Vertical sizing runs clip then vertical scale; horizontal sizing runs clip, non-square filter, horizontal scale.
- Established ([mace] PDF 13): mipmap generation starts from a field of 512 by 256 or 512 by 128 and halves both axes down eight levels by simple averaging (from 512 by 256: 256 by 128 and so on to 4 by 2). The revision history says the smallest level is 2 by 1; see the conflicts below. It works only with the 16-bit 5551 pixel format and needs at least 138 spare pixels per line and two spare lines at the bottom of the field ([mace] PDF 49).
- Established ([mace] PDF 14, 24-26): output conversion unpacks the pixels, optionally applies a notch filter (weights half, zero, half), converts to YUV, then subsamples chroma with a quarter, half, quarter filter. The up-sampling of missing chroma on input is the average of the two neighbours. Subsampling and up-sampling are automatic and cannot be selected.
- Established ([mace] PDF 24-25): RGB to YUV uses the integer approximation `Y = (263 R + 516 G + 100 B + 16896) / 1024` (and the two chroma equations on PDF 25), which maps 0-255 RGB to 16-235 luma and 16-240 chroma. YUV to RGB uses `R = (1192 Y + 1634 V - 227712) / 1024` and its two siblings, then clamps to 0-255. Both include 512 for rounding.
- Established ([mace] PDF 25-26): dither applies only to the 16-bit 5551 input format, is ordered, runs per field and is enabled by one bit; the 4 by 4 matrix is on PDF 26. The dither can never overflow a pixel.

## Pixel and buffer formats

- Established ([mace] PDF 27, 37, 40): big-endian only. The six DMA format codes in the configuration registers are 0 OpenGL RGBA 32-bit, 1 16-bit 1:5:5:5, 2 VL 4:2:2 YUV 8-bit, 3 the same at 10 bits, 4 OpenGL ABGR 32-bit and 5 VL 4:2:2:4 YUVA. The 10-bit format keeps ten bits only on 10-bit D1 and bypasses all processing except clipping; 10-bit components are left-aligned in 16 bits.
- Established ([mace] PDF 28-31): buffers are linear, tiled or (input only) mipmapped; capture is by field or interleaved frame; pages are 64 KB "super pages", and 4 KB pages are not supported. Tiles are 512 bytes wide, so 512, 256 or 128 pixels at one, two or four bytes, the same layout the rendering engine uses; a mipmapped tile adds a fourth tile holding the levels below the first three. A video line splits at a page boundary and continues on the next page.
- Established ([mace] PDF 28): a full PAL field of 768 by 288 four-byte pixels needs about 14 super-page descriptors; this is why each DMA descriptor holds 32 page entries. The 64 KB page and 512 by 128 tile match the CRIME tile of [spec] and [linux] `gbefb.c`, which is independent support for those sizes.

## Registers

All registers are 64-bit, big-endian doubleword accesses. The layouts below are recorded in [MACE_VIDEO_IN_DECODE.h](../MACE_VIDEO_IN_DECODE.h) for the input channel and [MACE_VIDEO_DECODE.h](../MACE_VIDEO_DECODE.h) for the output channel; fields are decoded for the control and status registers and the input configuration register, and the other registers are listed by offset only. A register marked double-buffered takes effect at the next field, when the next-descriptor register is read. Offsets are within one channel's set; the input set repeats at `+0x80000`.

### Input channel

- `VIN.CONTROL` at `+0x0`: established ([mace] PDF 34-35). Enables the DMA engine in bit 0, where writing zero resets the engine, and enables interrupts for vertical sync, DMA complete, lost sync, buffer, horizontal, vertical and FIFO overflow, CRIME memory error and the general-purpose input in bits 1 to 9.
- `VIN.STATUS` at `+0x8`: established ([mace] PDF 36-37). Reading returns the pending causes and clears them; DMA active (bit 0) and sync present (bit 10) reflect live state and are not cleared. Reading twice shows what arrived since the first read.
- `VIN.CONFIG` at `+0x10`: established ([mace] PDF 37-39). Channel reset, D1 reset, source select (primary, secondary, or loopback from port E or F), 8 or 10 bit D1 marker detection, D1 error correction, field-start line count, pixel format, dither, memory mode and interleave. The channel powers up in reset and must be in reset while any of these change.
- `VIN.NEXT_DESC` at `+0x18`: established ([mace] PDF 44). The 64-byte-aligned address of the descriptor table, a valid bit that hardware clears after copying, and a two-bit choice of which field to start on. Double-buffered; a clear valid bit skips the next field.
- `VIN.FLD_OFFSET` at `+0x20`: established ([mace] PDF 45). Byte offset of the first pixel within the first page, bits 15:3, so it is 8-byte aligned in linear mode and 512-byte aligned in tiled mode. Double-buffered.
- `VIN.LINE_WIDTH` at `+0x28`: established ([mace] PDF 45). Bytes per video line in bits 11:3. In interleaved linear mode it is added at each line end to leave a gap for the other field. Double-buffered.
- `VIN.HCLIP_ODD` at `+0x30`: established ([mace] PDF 46-47). Horizontal start and end pixel counted from the end-of-active-video code, the non-square filter count and mode, and the horizontal scale ratio (an eight-bit value, the ratio times 256) and enable. Clipping lands on even pixels only.
- `VIN.VCLIP_ODD` at `+0x38`: established ([mace] PDF 48-49). Vertical start, end and blank-fill line counted from start of field, plus the vertical scale ratio and enable. The black-fill field pads the top of a mipmapped field that is shorter than 128 or 256 lines.
- `VIN.ALPHA_ODD` at `+0x40`: inferred from the register table ([mace] PDF 33). An eight-bit alpha value for RGBA input pixels on odd lines; the spec gives no further text.
- `VIN.HCLIP_EVEN` at `+0x48`: established ([mace] PDF 45-47). The even-field twin of the odd horizontal clip register. Two sets let software change the region smoothly by editing the set not in use.
- `VIN.VCLIP_EVEN` at `+0x50`: established ([mace] PDF 45, 48). The even-field twin of the odd vertical clip register, with the same smooth-change rule.
- `VIN.ALPHA_EVEN` at `+0x58`: inferred ([mace] PDF 33). Eight-bit alpha for even-line RGBA pixels, by pairing with the odd register.
- `VIN.DMA_DESC` at `+0x80` to `+0xb8`: established ([mace] PDF 56). Eight doublewords holding thirty-two 16-bit page numbers, the upper 16 bits of a 64 KB page address. In linear mode a zero entry ends the field and raises a buffer overflow; in tiled mode it means that tile size is not wanted.

### Output channel

- `VOUT.CONTROL` at `+0x0`: established ([mace] PDF 34). Same layout as the input control register but with genlock-lost in bit 3, underflow in bit 7, and bits 5 and 6 unused.
- `VOUT.STATUS` at `+0x8`: established ([mace] PDF 36). The causes clear on read as on input, with FIFO underflow in bit 7; bit 9 and bit 10 do not exist.
- `VOUT.CONFIG` at `+0x10`: established ([mace] PDF 40-44). Channel reset, source of ports E and F (off, genlock pass-through, YUV or alpha), genlock reset, genlock source, 8 or 10 bit marker detection, error correction, start-of-field counts for odd and even, pixel format, notch filter, clamping mode, interleave and memory mode (linear or tiled only). Genlock reset must be released to produce any output, even free-running.
- `VOUT.NEXT_DESC` at `+0x18`: established ([mace] PDF 44). As on input, except bit 1 is unused and bit 0 says whether the next field goes out as odd or even.
- `VOUT.FLD_OFFSET` at `+0x20`: established ([mace] PDF 45). Same field as the input register.
- `VOUT.FIELD_SIZE` at `+0x28`: established ([mace] PDF 45). Lines per field in bits 21:12 and bytes per line in bits 11:3; the line must be a multiple of 8 bytes. Double-buffered: a new value takes effect at the next field (revision history item 46, PDF 59).
- `VOUT.HPAD_ODD` at `+0x30`: established ([mace] PDF 51-52). Horizontal timing in pixel pairs: end-of-active to start-of-active codes, end to end, end to first image pixel, plus the square-to-non-square filter count and mode (PAL 11/12, NTSC 11/10, or double width). Hardware inserts the end code always, the start code unless the image data carries it, and pads black after the image.
- `VOUT.VPAD_ODD` at `+0x38`: established ([mace] PDF 52). Vertical timing in lines: start of field to end of blanking, to start of image, to start of final blanking and to end of field. Black fill follows the image to the start of blanking.
- `VOUT.HPAD_EVEN` at `+0x40`: established ([mace] PDF 51). The even-field copy of the odd horizontal timing register.
- `VOUT.VPAD_EVEN` at `+0x48`: established ([mace] PDF 52). The even-field copy of the odd vertical timing register.
- `VOUT.GEN_DLY` at `+0x50`: established ([mace] PDF 52-53). Extra genlock delay of up to a line in bits 10:0, in clocks; it must never be zero when a genlock source is used. The fixed delay is 15 clocks from a D1 source and 10 from the external sync, so the minimum totals are 16 and 11.
- `VOUT.VHW_CFG` at `+0x58`: established ([mace] PDF 53-55). Chip-level video setup that belongs to neither channel: reset and AB/CD select for the two input ports, the audio-sync source with its precision and correction bits, the same set for GBE framelock, the general-purpose output pin in bit 18, and a four-bit chip revision code in bits 35:32. Recorded in [MACE_VIDEO_DECODE.h](../MACE_VIDEO_DECODE.h).
- `VOUT.DMA_DESC` at `+0x80` to `+0xb8`: established ([mace] PDF 56). Same format as the input descriptors, linear or tiled only.

The counter that stamps each field lives in the MACE timer block, not here: its low bit says odd (0) or even (1) field and its upper half snapshots the system uptime at start of field ([mace] PDF 56). Established.

The three video pairs sit at timer offsets `0x38` (input 1), `0x40` (input 2) and `0x48` (output), and an aligned 64-bit read of each returns the same layout ([mace] PDF 56, table 25 and PDF 148). The count advances at field end while a real video signal is active. DMA enable does not clock it, and software can write it (established, [mace] PDF 56). The timer chapter's table on PDF 148 names UST at the pair base and MSC at base plus four, the reverse of this layout, while its atomic-read rule agrees with the layout here. What a 32-bit access to one half does is not published, so this chapter does not settle it.

### Field geometry, clipping and reset domains

- Established ([mace] PDF 46-49, tables 17 and 18): horizontal clip start and end count pixels after the end-of-active code, and the first captured pixel is the stored start plus two. Vertical clip start and end count lines from the first line after decoded start of field, and capture runs from start through end inclusive. The effect of the black-fill boundary is a reviewed interpretation and is not recorded here.
- Established ([mace] PDF 51-52, tables 20 and 21): output raster totals count from the end-of-active code. Horizontal fields are in pixel pairs: total raster pixels per line are 2 times (value plus 1), and the pixels before the image are 2 times (value plus 1). Vertical fields store counts minus one.
- Established ([mace] PDF 45, tables 15 and 16): input and output line widths are literal byte counts in multiples of eight, and the field masks admit 8 to 4088 bytes. Output line counts run from 1 to 1023 with no plus one.
- Established ([mace] PDF 28-30, 45): linear addressing starts at the programmed first-page offset. The chip adds no parity-dependent initial line offset, so software sets a separate offset for each field. Successive lines advance through the descriptor's 64 KiB pages. Interleaved mode adds one programmed line width after each line.
- Established ([mace] PDF 37, 42): input and output processing resets do not reset the DMA section. Input D1 reset resets only the decoding circuitry. Output and genlock reset hold the clocked output circuitry, free-running output included, in reset.
- Established ([mace] PDF 35): input completion means the final bytes have reached memory. Output completion means the final bytes have left the output FIFO or sample stream.
- Established as prose ([mace] PDF 34-35, 56): the chapter describes zero-descriptor termination and a stop on FIFO error. It publishes no single recovery sequence, so what software must do after either error is unknown.

## Driver access

How the 6.5.30 video kernel driver `mvp.o` reaches the hardware, from the routines read so far. Labels as above; offsets are bytes. Sites are `mvp.o` text addresses. The driver keeps one controller record, found through the word `mvpcontroller_table`, and reaches every register through pointers stored in it.

- Established (`mvpreg_init`, `mvp.o` `0xbdb0`, 188 bytes): it stores five KSEG1 pointers into the controller record it is given in a0, as words: `0xbf100000` at record `+192` (`0xbdb4`, input channel 1), `0xbf180000` at `+196` (`0xbdbc`, input channel 2), `0xbf200000` at `+200` (`0xbdc4`, the output channel), `0xb6080000` at `+204` (`0xbdcc`, the GBE capture block, GBE offset `0x80000`) and `0xbf330000` at `+208` (`0xbdd8`, a block of the MACE peripheral space at `0x1f330000` that the audio driver also reaches, see [functions.md](functions.md)). The inventory configuration names `+192`, `+200` and `+204` for the routines read so far, and `+208`, the base of the MACE two-wire bus interface, under the window `MACEI2C` ([Two-wire bus interface](#two-wire-bus-interface)).
- Established (the instructions): the per-channel state records start at record `+216` and are 192 bytes apart, so channel `n` (0, 1 or 2) has its register pointer at `+192 + 4n` and its state record at `+216 + 192n`. The first doubleword of each state record is a shadow of the channel's `+0x0` control register; the routines below keep it equal to the last value they wrote to the register.
- The routines that take the channel from a path record (the byte at path `+1132`) load the pointer through `+192 + 4n`, so one instruction covers input 1, input 2 and the output; the inventory lists those accesses under the window `MACEVCH`, the offsets of one channel's own `0x80000`-byte register set. Routines that name the output channel or the capture block load `+200` or `+204` directly and appear under `MACEV` (output offsets from `0x100000`) and `GBE` (from `0x80000`).

- Established (`mvpinterrupt`, `mvpinterrupt_init`, `mvpinterrupt_unload`, [functions.md](functions.md)): the driver reaches CRIME through the data word at `.data` `+0xa20`, `0xb4000000`. It reads `INTSTAT` (`+0x10`) in `mvpinterrupt` (`0x96a0`) and `INTMASK` (`+0x18`) in `mvpinterrupt_init` and `mvpinterrupt_unload`, the latter with the value discarded. The inventory configuration names the word as a global of window `CRIME`.

### Control, status and next-descriptor registers

- `VIN.CONTROL` and `VOUT.CONTROL` at `+0x0` (window `MACEVCH` `+0x0`, `MACEV` `+0x100000`): established from the instructions of the routines below. `mvptransfer_stop_dma` (`0x87c`) clears the low nine bits, `& -512` at `0x8e8`, of the channel's shadow and writes the result (`0x8f0`); those nine bits are the DMA enable and eight interrupt enables ([mace] PDF 34-35). `mvptransfer_enablechannel` (`0x17d8`) replaces the low nine bits with the low nine bits of its second argument (`0x1840`) and writes the shadow and then the register (`0x1860`) only when the value changed; an argument of -1 leaves the register as it is. `mvpinterrupt_clear` (`0x95c4`) clears the same nine bits and writes the register in the delay slot of its return (`0x95fc`). For the output channel, `mvptransfer_fix_output` (`0x21cc`) clears bit 0, `0x2218`, and sets it again, `0x2234`, around a clear of `+0x18`; `mvptransfer_cleardma` (`0x226c`) clears bit 0 (`0x22d0`), sets it (`0x22e0`) and clears it again (`0x22f4`, in the delay slot of the status read).
- `VIN.STATUS` and `VOUT.STATUS` at `+0x8` (`MACEVCH` `+0x8`, `MACEV` `+0x100008`): established. `mvpinterrupt_clear` reads the channel's status doubleword at `0x95ec` before it clears the enables and discards the value; reading clears the pending causes ([mace] PDF 36-37). `mvptransfer_cleardma` calls `pciio_pio_read64` on the output status address (`+0x8`, call at `0x22f0`) after its last control write, which sits in the call's delay slot, and discards the value.
- `VOUT.NEXT_DESC` at `+0x18` (`MACEV` `+0x100018`) and `VIN.NEXT_DESC` (`MACEVCH` `+0x18`): established. `mvptransfer_start_dma` (`0x524`) writes a doubleword to the channel's `+0x18` at `0x70c`: a zero-extended word, the physical address of a block of the path record (`kvtophys` of path plus 64 times the byte at `+170` of the transfer record) combined with a flag byte from the path (`+1139` or `+1140`). `mvptransfer_fix_output` and `mvptransfer_cleardma` write zero to the output register (`0x221c`, `0x22d4`), and `mvptransfer_fix_output` then writes the word at path `+1160`, zero-extended, at `0x2254`.
- `FLD_OFFSET` at `+0x20` (`MACEVCH` `+0x20`, `MACEV` `+0x100020`): established. `mvptransfer_start_dma` writes a doubleword at `0x6f8` (the low halfword of the word at `+136` of the transfer record), only when it differs from the shadow; `mvptransfer_cleardma` writes zero to the output register at `0x22c0` only when its shadow was nonzero; `mvpreg_initmace` writes the table value 0 to it (below).

### Defaults written by `mvpreg_initmace`

- Established (`mvpreg_initmace`, `mvp.o` `0xb6a8`, 536 bytes, read in full): its second argument selects the case, 0 or 1 an input channel, 2 the output channel, 3 the GBE capture block, and any other value does nothing; it raises the interrupt level (`splhi`) first and restores it on every path. Bit 0 of its third argument chooses one of two sets of constants held in the driver's data section, and its fourth argument, when nonzero, skips the write to the `+0x10` configuration register. Each value is stored to the register and to a copy in the controller record.
- Input channel (case 0 or 1; first set at data `0xa38`, second at `0xab0`): the registers are written in this order. The doubleword at table `+0` goes to `+0x30` (`VIN.HCLIP_ODD`, `0xb758`) and `+0x48` (`VIN.HCLIP_EVEN`, `0xb760`); table `+8` to `+0x38` (`VIN.VCLIP_ODD`, `0xb76c`); table `+16` to `+0x50` (`VIN.VCLIP_EVEN`, `0xb778`); table `+24` to `+0x28` (`VIN.LINE_WIDTH`, `0xb784`); table `+32` to `+0x40` (`VIN.ALPHA_ODD`, `0xb790`) and `+0x58` (`VIN.ALPHA_EVEN`, `0xb798`); table `+40` to `+0x20` (`VIN.FLD_OFFSET`, `0xb7a8`); and, unless the fourth argument is nonzero, table `+48` to `+0x10` (`VIN.CONFIG`, `0xb7b8`). The two sets differ in the clip words (`0x2d0d5c88` against `0x2d0d748e` for the horizontal word at `+0`), the vertical words and the configuration word (`0x100e3` against `0x100a3`); both set the line width to `0xb40` (2880) bytes and both alpha words to `0xff`.
- Output channel (case 2; first set at data `0xa70`, second at `0xae8`): table `+0` goes to `+0x30` (`VOUT.HPAD_ODD`, `0xb840`) and `+0x40` (`VOUT.HPAD_EVEN`, `0xb848`); table `+8` to `+0x38` (`VOUT.VPAD_ODD`, `0xb854`); table `+16` to `+0x48` (`VOUT.VPAD_EVEN`, `0xb860`); table `+24` to `+0x28` (`VOUT.FIELD_SIZE`, `0xb86c`); table `+32` to `+0x20` (`VOUT.FLD_OFFSET`, `0xb87c`); and, unless the fourth argument is nonzero, table `+40` to `+0x50` (`VOUT.GEN_DLY`, `0xb88c`) and table `+48` to `+0x10` (`VOUT.CONFIG`, `0xb8a4`). The field-size words are `0xf3b40` in the first set and `0x120b40` in the second, which are 243 and 288 lines of 2880 bytes. Inferred: the two sets are the 525-line and 625-line standards, from the line counts alone; no string or caller names them.
- GBE capture block (case 3): stores zero to all nine registers `VC_0` to `VC_8` (`+0x80000` to `+0x80020` of GBE, sites `0xb7cc` to `0xb810`) and zero to nine words of the controller record from `+792`.
- Established (`mvpreg_setup_timing`, `0xb8c0`, 36 bytes): calls `mvpreg_initmace` with its own first three arguments and a fourth of 1, so a timing change leaves the configuration register alone. `mvpreg_cdto_wait` (`0xb8e4`, 44 bytes) calls `delay` with the word `mvpcdto_wait_time` and returns 1.
- Established (`mvpreg_init`, continued): when `mvpavio_preset_status` returns -1 it calls `mvpreg_initmace` for cases 0, 1, 2 and 3 in that order, each with the byte at controller `+98` as third argument and 0 as fourth; in every case it ends by returning the result of `mvpreg_setpassthru` called with 1 and 1.

### The GBE capture block and `VHW_CFG`

- `VC_0` to `VC_3` at GBE `+0x80000` to `+0x8000c`: established. `mvptransfer_stop_dma` clears `VC_0` to `VC_3` (zero words at `0x920`, `0x928`, `0x930`, `0x93c`) for a path whose type byte, path `+1192`, is 2, after zeroing four words of the controller record from `+792`; `mvpreg_initmace` case 3 clears them too. `mvptransfer_start_dma` writes `VC_3` at `0x7e4` and sets bit 0 of `VC_2`, the DMA enable, at `0x7fc` (the old value comes from the record copy at `+800`, so the register is written only when the bit was clear).

When the driver word at data `+8` is nonzero, `mvptransfer_start_dma` copies five words from an uncached view of the physical descriptor block into them (`0x7bc` to `0x7e0`) before it writes `VC_3`; the word written to `VC_3` is then the byte at path `+1139` or `+1140` alone, otherwise it is that byte combined with the descriptor block's physical address. This replaces the earlier statement that no shipped software drives the block ([gbe.md](gbe.md)).
- `VOUT.VHW_CFG` at `+0x58` (`MACEV` `+0x100058`): established for the read. `mvpprobe` (`0x7348`, 48 bytes) reads the doubleword at `0xbf200058` through `pciio_pio_read64` (`0x735c`) and returns bits 35:32, the chip revision code, by shifting right 32 and masking with 15. ### The writers of `VHW_CFG`

Established (`mvp.o`, from the instructions of `mvpreg_setpassthru`, `mvpreg_syncpath_controls` and `mvpreg_syncdevice_controls`, entries in [functions.md](functions.md)); bit meanings from [mace] PDF 40-44 and 53-55. Each routine keeps the word in the output state record at controller `+688` and stores it to `+0x58` only when it differs from that shadow. The stores are at `0xbb48` (`mvpreg_setpassthru`), `0xc220` (`mvpreg_syncpath_controls`) and `0xc6b0` and `0xc760` (`mvpreg_syncdevice_controls`).

- `VOUT.CONFIG` at `+0x10` (`MACEV` `+0x100010`): `mvpreg_setpassthru` builds the word with the format at 4:2:2 YUV 8 bit, the genlock reset released and the start-of-field counts 3 (odd) and 2 (even). Ports E and F take `01` (pass the genlock input through) when the output follows an input and `10` (the YUV stream) when it is generated, and the genlock source field (bits 7:6) is port CD for input indexes 1, 4 and 6, port AB for 2, 3 and 5 and none for 7 (stores at `0xbb5c`). `mvpreg_syncdevice_controls` takes the E and F sources, the notch bit and the genlock source from a settings record and writes the word twice when a genlock field changed, first with the genlock reset (bit 5) low (`0xc850`) and then high (`0xc868`).
- `VOUT.GEN_DLY` at `+0x50` (`MACEV` `+0x100050`): `mvpreg_syncdevice_controls` writes the halfword at settings `+26` to it (`0xc8bc`) when it differs from the shadow at controller `+680`. The register holds the added genlock delay in clocks, bits 10:0 ([mace] PDF 52-53); the routine does not test the rule that it must not be zero with a genlock source.
- `VHW_CFG` bits the routines drive: the Port AB and Port CD resets (bits 0 and 2), released whenever an input or output is set up; the AB and CD select bits (1 and 3), from the input index or from path bytes; the audio-sync reset, source (bits 6:5), precision and correction (bits 4, 7 and 8); the GBE framelock reset (bit 9), source (11:10), precision (12), correction (13) and start-of-field counts (17:14 odd, 22:19 even); and the general-purpose output bit 18, from bit 0 of a settings byte. Where a routine sets the framelock counts the even count is 2 and the odd count 2 or 3. When the output is generated, `mvpreg_setpassthru` selects external sync (`01`) as the framelock source with its reset held; `mvpreg_syncdevice_controls` takes the source from a settings byte and releases the reset unless that byte is 8.
- Established (`mvpavio_preset_input`, `_output` and `_genlock`, [functions.md](functions.md)): the input index at controller `+97` is 1 camera, 2 composite, 3 svideo, 4 digital, 5 primary, 6 secondary and 7 loopback; the preset output is 0 black, 1 image and 2 colorbars; the preset genlock is 0 internal, 1 external, 2 analog or primary and 3 digital or secondary. They come from boot-argument strings. `mvpreg_setpassthru` clears the select bits (1 and 3 of `VHW_CFG`) for every input index, so the Port AB and Port CD select bits are set only from the path bytes at `+3044` and `+3045` by `mvpreg_syncpath_controls`.
- Unknown: what decides the odd framelock start-of-field count, which a small local routine at `0xc564` answers per source. Routes tried: the three writers and the `mvpavio_*` layer. Next: that routine.

### Two-wire bus interface

The pointer at controller `+208` and at object `+16` of the two video-port chip objects is `0xbf330000`, the KSEG1 address of the MACE peripheral controller's sub-block 3, the two-wire (I2C) bus interface ([mace] PDF 149-151 and the peripheral sub-address map, PDF 181). The driver reaches it through `pciio_pio_read64` and doubleword stores. The window `MACEI2C` of the inventory covers it; it also holds the sites of the audio driver `a3_dd.o`, which reaches the same bus through the constant `0xbf330010` ([functions.md](functions.md)).

- `+0x0` (`MACEI2C`, configuration and reset): established (`mvp.o`). Three routines write it, and `mvpi2c_config` also reads it.
  `mvpi2c_reset` (`0x8660`) returns without writing when a word in the driver's data (`+32` of the block at data `0x9dc`) is nonzero. Otherwise it writes 1, bit 0 (reset active), as a doubleword at `0x8690`.
  After a delay taken from the chip object, it writes 2 (bit 1, 400 kHz fast mode enable) when bit 3 of the byte at the chip object's `+11` is set, otherwise 0, at `0x8704`.
  `mvpi2c_fastmode` (`0x8718`) writes 0 at `0x8730` and sets the rate halfword at `+12` to 5, or writes 2 at `0x8754` and sets it to 1.
  `mvpi2c_config` (`0x856c`) reads the register with a call to `pciio_pio_read64` at `0x8590` and writes it back at `0x8628`. For each of the low four bits that its mask argument (`a1`) selects, the bit takes the value of the matching bit of its value argument (`a2`). The four bits are reset, fast mode, data-pin override and clock-pin override ([mace] PDF 151). Bits 4 and 5 are read-only levels of the data and clock lines, with no fixed reset value (established, [mace] PDF 151, table 79).
  `mvpi2c_read_config` (`0x8638`) passes its arguments to `pciio_pio_read64` (call at `0x8648`). No direct call to it appears in `mvp.o`, so its caller sets the offset. It returns 0 whatever the read gave (`0x8654`).
- `+0x10` (`MACEI2C`, control and status): established (`mvp.o` and `a3_dd.o`). `mvpi2c_check` (`0x8770`) reads it through `pciio_pio_read64` (the call at `0x8804`; its address argument is set in the delay slot at `0x8808`). The transfer routines read it and test bit 4 (transfer status: `_mvpi2c_wait_for_xfer_done` at `0x886c` loops while it is set), bit 5 (acknowledge status: `_mvpi2c_wait_for_ack` at `0x89c8` and `0x8a24` loops while it is set), bit 0 (not idle: `_mvpi2c_force_bus_idle` at `0x8b20` and `0x8bcc`) and bit 7 (bus error: `_mvpi2c_force_bus_idle` at `0x8c58`), with the names from [mace] PDF 150. The same routines write it: `_mvpi2c_force_bus_idle` stores its control argument (5 from `mvpi2c_read_reg`), with bit 0 cleared when the bus was not idle (`0x8b44`); `mvpi2c_read_reg` and `mvpi2c_write_reg` store 1 after the address phase (`0x8e4c`, `0x92a8`) and `mvpi2c_read_reg` stores 3 before the read (`0x8f78`, `0x8e94`) and 0 after it. Per [mace] PDF 149 and 150 bit 1 is the bus direction (0 for write) and bit 2 the last-byte control (0 for the last byte), so 1 is a write of a last byte, 3 starts a read and 5 is a write with more bytes to follow; [mace] PDF 150 (table 77) states these meanings directly, so the reading is established; the stores themselves are established as well. Bit 0 written as one has no effect, bits 3 and 6 are reserved and read-only, and bits 4, 5 and 7 are read-only status.
- `+0x18` (`MACEI2C`, data): established (`mvp.o` and `a3_dd.o`). In `mvp.o` `mvpi2c_read_reg` (`0x8cbc`) and `mvpi2c_write_reg` (`0x9098`) store through it the chip address byte (from chip record `+14`, with bit 0 set for a read cycle), the register number and the data byte, each followed by `_mvpi2c_wait_for_ack`; `mvpi2c_read_reg` reads it back after the transfer-status wait and returns the byte. `mvpi2c_init_regs` (`0x92ec`) has no store to it; it calls `mvpi2c_write_reg` and stores the control word at `+0x10` itself. The register is the bus data register of [mace] PDF 150.
- `+0x8` (`MACEI2C`, alias): established ([mace] PDF 150, table 76). The table lists this slot as reserved and notes that it aliases the configuration register, bits 5:0 only. This section records no access to it.

### Published scope and open points

These points come from the published chapter ([mace] PDF 149 to 151 and the address map on PDF 181). The register and field grades are in `devices/mace_i2c/registers.toml`.

- Established ([mace] PDF 149): the bus is an open-collector party line with several masters, and collision detection is in hardware. Address recognition, backoff and retry after a collision, and remote reset are not in hardware. Software does them.
- Established ([mace] PDF 149): the chapter supports only the 400 kHz and 100 kHz rates, selected by configuration bit 1.
- Established ([mace] PDF 149 and 150): the transmit sequence is to read control bit 0 to test for idle, write zero to bit 0 to request the bus, and wait for idle, with a timeout of more than 400 microseconds. Then set the direction to write, and write each byte with the hold bit set to one, except that the last byte is written with the hold bit at zero to release the bus. The status bits are read afterward.
- Established absence ([mace] PDF 125): the peripheral interrupt table names no two-wire cause. Software polls the status bits, and no interrupt or DMA path from this block is described.
- Conflict ([mace] PDF 150, tables 77 and 78): table 78 says a write starts a write cycle when control bit 1 is one. Table 77 says bit 1 set means read. The write case is the only point where the tables disagree. This section follows table 77, the bit-1 table, for the reading of the stored values above.
- Unknown: the chapter says the chip has two I2C interfaces for external codecs (PDF 149). The register table and the address map describe one block and no selection bit (PDF 150 and 181). Whether a second interface exists on the O2, and where it sits, is not established.
- Unknown: the chapter does not say how to issue a repeated START. Its procedure releases the bus between transfers and gives no combined write-then-read without a STOP. Whether the hold bit can carry one across an address change is not stated.

### The register test in the standalone diagnostics

Established (`ide.IP32`, 6.5.30, `MACE I2C Register Test` at `0xa00d03e0`): the test stores `0xbf330000`, the same two-wire base, in a global at `0xa032b8b0` (the store is at `0xa00d0414`), zeroes its error count, calls five routines in this order and reports `i2c_reg PASSED` for a zero count or `i2c_reg FAILED ( %d errors)` otherwise. All its accesses are doubleword loads and stores at `+0` and `+0x10`.

- `i2c_reset` (`0xa00cf260`) reads `+0` and `+0x10`, then writes `+0` with the value it just read plus bit 0 set, re-reads both, and counts an error (`CRR RESET cannot be set`) when bit 0 of the re-read configuration word is clear. Just before that it writes 0 to `+0` and reads it again, unless a word at `0xa0221b08` is non-zero. That word is zero in the image and the only store to it is the test's own zero store, so the branches that would require bit 0 to read clear on entry (`CRR RESET incorrect reset value`) are not reached.
- `chk_reset_csr` (`0xa00cf6a8`) and `chk_reset_crr` (`0xa00cf4c4`) then require zero in each tested bit: bits 3 to 1 of `+0` and bits 0, 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7 of `+0x10`. They run after `i2c_reset` and do not test bit 0 of `+0`. The messages name the fields in bit order, fast mode enable, data pin override and clock pin override for `+0`, and force idle state control, bus direction control, last byte control, transfer status, acknowledge status and bus error status for `+0x10`; reading them against the bits is inferred from that order and agrees with the field names used above.
- `chk_toggle_csr` (`0xa00cfa00`) sets and then clears bit 1 and bit 2 of `+0x10`, and `chk_toggle_crr` (`0xa00cfdf0`) sets and then clears bits 1, 2 and 3 of `+0`. Each write is followed by a read-back, and a bit that does not follow counts an error (`CSR BDC`, `CSR LBC`, `CRR FME`, `CRR DPO` or `CRR CPO cannot be set` or `cannot be cleared`).

So the test never assumes a state at entry: it resets the block first and then compares against all-zero values, so a warm entry from IRIX and a cold power-on read the same values unless the block keeps bits across a reset command. A model passes when the two registers read back as doublewords, the reset bit reads back as written, bits 3 to 1 of `+0` and the listed bits of `+0x10` read zero after it, and bits 1 to 3 of `+0` and bits 1 and 2 of `+0x10` hold what is written. An unmodelled register fails the toggle or the zero checks. What a real MACE returns after a warm entry is not decided by this code and no other shipped software depends on it.

### Timer block

The word at `.data` +0 of `mvp.o` is `0xbf340000`, the KSEG1 address of the MACE count and compare timer block, sub-block 4 of the peripheral controller ([mace] PDF 181, table 92). The window `MACETIMER` of the inventory covers it. The driver reads it through `pciio_pio_read64` only.

- `+0x0` (`MACETIMER`, universal system time): established (`mvp.o`, `mvptransfer_getustmsc` at `0x1884`, call at `0x19c8`). It is the fall-back read of the routine's channel-3 path when `crime_getustmsc` fails: one doubleword read, whose low word and the high word one greater than the stored word at `+84` of the controller table entry go through the same wrap handling as a channel snapshot. Register name from [mace] PDF 148, table 75; the use as a fall-back is read from the instructions.
- `+0x38`, `+0x40` and `+0x48` (`MACETIMER`, video input 1, video input 2 and video output snapshots): established (`mvptransfer_getustmsc`, call at `0x18cc`). The address is the timer base plus 8 times the channel number (the byte at transfer record `+1132`, 0 to 2) plus 56, a doubleword read. The routine splits it (`0x18d4` to `0x1950`): the high word is extended by a wrap counter kept in the controller table entry (the word at `+32` is incremented when the new high word is below the one stored at `+36`) and the 64-bit result goes to `+8` of the routine's output; the low word goes through `fill_ust_highbits` and then `ust_to_nano`, and the result goes to `+0`. What the two outputs are called by the caller was not read, so which of the two words the driver treats as the time stamp and which as the field count is unknown here; the table of [mace] PDF 148 lists the snapshot pair as UST at the first word and MSC at the second (inferred as the high and low words of a big-endian doubleword). The inventory counts these sites as unresolved because the offset depends on the channel.

## Agreement and conflicts with what this repository already holds

- Agreement: the window addresses and sizes in the inventory configuration match [mace] table 4 once the base is `0x1f000000` (above). The video interrupt inputs match [spec] table 5-5.
- Agreement: the CRIME tile and the video tile are the same 64 KB, 512-byte-wide unit.
- Established (`mvpSetScale` at `0x407d90`, N32 `usr/lib32/dmedia/video/mvp.so`, 824 bytes; read 2026-11-10): the library's horizontal scaler set-up takes a first size, a second size and a halve flag. Equal sizes return 0x100 (no scaling, the value the clip register's 8-bit ratio field cannot hold). Otherwise a halve flag first halves both sizes, a negative size is made positive and flips a mirror flag, and the ratio is `trunc(second / first * 256.0 + 0.5)` in single precision. A ratio that is not in 1 to 255 returns -1 (refused), so a second size of 99.8 percent of the first or more, including any enlargement, is refused: only reductions reach the register. The routine then nudges the ratio up or down until `ratio / 2 + ratio * (first - 2) + 0x100` is within one step of `second * 256` (the accumulated-position test; the constants are as read, their meaning is inferred from the ratio field being the reduction ratio times 256, [mace] PDF 46), prints a debug line when the library's debug flag is set, and returns the ratio negated if the mirror flag was set. This is the scale-down-only limit of the 8-bit field made visible in software: the lead's "no scale-up" holds for the MACE scaler path (established for the routine; that the video-zoom-size control reaches it is inferred, because the routine is the only ratio computer in the library and is called from the control-setting code at `0x41087c` to `0x4108cc`).
- Conflict inside [mace]: the horizontal clip register text on PDF 46 says the PAL non-square filter scales "up by 7868/720", while the filter overview (PDF 13-14, 21) and the output register (PDF 51) say 12/11, 786 pixels. 720 times 12/11 is about 785, so 786 is the reading; `7868` is read as a misprint. Inferred. Shipped software gives no second source (searched 2026-11-10: no halfword or word with value 786 or 7868 at an aligned position in N32 `mvp.so`, kernel `mvp.o` or `libXsgivc.so`, and no immediate load of either value in `mvp.so`; the NTSC value 654 does appear, as the width of a record in the `mvp.so` format table at `0x1a54e`, and the PAL square-pixel records in the same table use 768 wide, not 786). So the shipped video library never programs the PAL non-square filter count, and nothing depends on the choice. Unknown: no shipped software or published source depends on it. What would settle it: read the filter-count field back from a real unit after enabling the PAL filter in a capture, or the 1996 specification's later revision.
- Conflict inside [mace]: the mipmap overview (PDF 13) lists levels from 512 by 256 down to 4 by 2. Revision history item 9 (PDF 57, dated 12/01/94) says the hardware generates mipmaps down to 2 by 1 and cannot make 1 by 1. Nothing in hand says which the silicon does. Unknown.
- Conflict inside [mace]: the overview gives the Mitchell non-square ratios as 10/11 and 12/11 (PDF 13-14, 21). The input clip table (PDF 46) gives NTSC 654/720 and PAL 7868/720 (see the misprint note above). Revision history item 45 (PDF 59, dated 6/10/96) reads "Change Mitchell Filter ratios", so the overview text may predate that change. Neither 654 nor 786 is exactly 720 times 10/11 or 12/11, so the exact ratio is unresolved.
- Conflict inside [mace]: the register summary (PDF 33) lists the video input and output status registers as RW. Every field in tables 8 and 9 (PDF 36) is RO, and the text says a read clears the pending causes while the live bits stay. This chapter follows the field tables and the read-to-clear rule; the status defs record the register as read-only.
