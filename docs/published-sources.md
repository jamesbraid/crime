# Published sources: patents, technical reports and white papers

What the published primary sources say about the O2 graphics and media chips: CRIME (the memory controller and rendering engine, called MRE in the product literature), GBE, MTE (the memory transfer engine inside CRIME) and the imaging and compression engine (ICE, VICE). One entry per source, each with its identity, what it covers, how closely it matches the shipped machine, and the facts it gives. Authors are left out on purpose; the title, venue or number identifies each source.

Labels follow [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md), with one addition for published material:

- **established (published)**: a first-party SGI report, white paper or paper about the shipped product says it. It is a designers' statement, not a read of the device or of the specification.
- **inferred**: a patent, or a paper about another product, says it. A patent describes a design and does not prove the shipped chip. A patent fact becomes established only where a binary or a stored test output agrees, and then the entry cites the binary and the owning chapter.
- **unknown**: the source does not say.

The CRIME 1.5 specification and the GBE specification, which this workspace already reads as [spec], are not repeated here. The MACE and VICE specifications are read in [mace-video.md](mace-video.md) and [vice.md](vice.md).

## 1. O2 Technical Report, version 1.0

- Identity: SGI, "O2 Technical Report", version 1.0, October 1997, 63 pages. Copy read: https://bukosek.si/hardware/collection/sgi-o2/o2-tech-report.pdf (SHA-256 `c0fad6ac1e1c982454c3b867c719bd6e62ef171cd0caa46f04ff2afc42c714ee`).
- Stored copy: [docs/sources/reports/o2-technical-report-1.0.pdf](sources/reports/o2-technical-report-1.0.pdf), SHA-256 `c0fad6ac1e1c982454c3b867c719bd6e62ef171cd0caa46f04ff2afc42c714ee`, library path `graphics/o2/o2-technical-report-1.0.pdf`.
- Covers: the whole machine. Chapter 3 memory, chapter 4 graphics (the rendering engine), chapter 7 imaging (ICE). The report calls CRIME the "MRE".
- Match: exact; product level, so no register or width below what is quoted.

Facts (established, published), each bearing on an entry of [pixel-arithmetic.md](pixel-arithmetic.md) or [coverage.md](coverage.md):

- **Position precision.** "6-bit subpixel positioning for OpenGL coordinates", a 4K x 4K address space for OpenGL coordinates and 64K x 64K for X coordinates; the rasteriser is "set up from vertex window coordinates" and rasterises points, lines, triangles and rectangles "atomically from their vertices". This confirms the 6 fraction bits of the vertex word that pixel-arithmetic.md reads as 13.6 and calls inferred; the 4K range is the 12 integer bits plus the bias bit. The report calls the line and triangle sampling "Bresenham sampling"; [pixel-arithmetic.md](pixel-arithmetic.md) reads plane evaluation at the integer corner. The two are not contradictory for lines but the word is the report's only statement about the triangle rule: it fixes no tie rule or half-pixel offset, so the corner-sampling reading stays inferred.
- **Set-up is on the host.** The rendering engine "offloads pre-rasterization functions (lighting, transforms, texture vertex assignments, plane equation set-up) to the CPU" and interpolates colour, z, texture coordinates and fog "from plane equation parameters set up by CPU". This agrees with the host-side set-up read in [pixel-arithmetic.md](pixel-arithmetic.md) (the driver computes the plane slopes and writes them).
- **Pipeline stages.** A deep single-pixel-wide pipeline modelled on OpenGL "from the rasterize stage forward": flat and Gouraud shading with RGBA and colour-index interpolation, 24-bit z interpolation with the OpenGL depth tests, 8-bit stencil, 1D and 2D textures with a per-pixel floating-point perspective divide, per-pixel mipmap level of detail and OpenGL mipmap and filter modes, fog values "linearly interpolated and applied to RGB colour", alpha test, alpha blending with all OpenGL functions, logic operations, colour plane masking. The "floating-point perspective divide" is the first statement that the s/w, t/w and 1/w steppers feed a floating-point divider; the width of the quotient is not given. The 24-bit z agrees with the 24-bit depth the stepper truncates to.
- **Dither.** "Dithering: supported for 3:3:2 and 5:5:5 RGB formats." This settles which formats the dither bit (bit 9 of DrawMode) acts on: the 8-bit and 16-bit colour formats, not 8:8:8:8. The specification's dithering section is "TBD" ([pixel-arithmetic.md](pixel-arithmetic.md)); this adds the format list, not the matrix. The dither matrix and the rounding remain unknown: no published source gives them.
- **Antialiasing.** Partial-pixel coverage is generated for lines only; antialiased lines are rasterised "as two-pixel-wide lines"; coverage "generated from slope- and distance-based coverage tables in ROM", with "filtered antialiased line endpoints"; coverage is applied to alpha; the host may supply a coverage value for any primitive. This agrees with the 2.14 slope and ideal-minor fields of the antialiased-line step ([context.md](context.md), table 7-3 of the specification). The ROM contents are in no published source; source 8 describes a design of the same kind. Polygons have no coverage stage in this list.
- **Formats.** Colour 3:3:2, 5:5:5:1, 8:8:8:8 in both channel orders, 8- and 12-bit colour index; texels 8-bit luminance or intensity, 1:5:5:5, 4:4:4:4, 16-bit luminance with 8-bit alpha, 8:8:8:8; buffers up to 2K x 2K; textures from 1K x 1K down to 1 x 1. The 1K maximum agrees with the format word's 1024 limit. The texel list is the first published one for the engine.
- **Tiling.** Pixel buffers are tiles of "128 rows of 512 bytes", 64 KB of physically contiguous memory, scattered through memory; used for colour, stencil and depth, offscreen and texture buffers. The report says the pixel buffer layout is used by OpenGL through the pbuffer structure.
- **Clears and copies.** "Copies and clears at up to full memory bandwidth", "full memory bandwidth byte-aligned flat rectangle fills", and pixel transfers (DMA) from a linear or tiled buffer to the frame buffer in which source pixels are format-converted (including YCrCb to RGB) and "sent through [the] rendering pipeline" with "simultaneous blending and copying". The last item is a pixel path through the rendering pipeline, not a plain copy; compare [mte.md](mte.md) and [pixel-transfer.md](pixel-transfer.md), which cover the transfer engine's copy and fill.
- **Memory.** Four banks of dual-interleaved DIMMs, 256 data bits (288 with ECC) every cycle at 66 MHz, 2.1 GB/s peak; CAS latency 2; 32 MB to 256 MB with 16 Mbit parts and 128 MB to 1 GB with 64 Mbit parts; ECC on all memory except frame buffers; a fixed priority arbitration: display engine, then audio and video, then ICE, then the rendering engine, then the CPU. The 16/64 Mbit choice is the bit 8 of the bank control registers ([spec] PDF 150, 152).
- **ICE (chapter 7).** A media signal processor with 6 KB of data RAM, 4 KB of instruction RAM, a 32-bit scalar unit, a 128-bit vector unit with one multiply-accumulate per clock (eight 16-bit or sixteen 8-bit multiplies), and dual issue; a bitstream processor with a 16-bit RISC-like load-store core, multi-cycle instruction extensions, table look-up for variable-length tokens (tables for JPEG) and factory Huffman tables, rated 60 MHz; two DMA channels with state machine, control registers and descriptor memory programmable by the host, the scalar unit or the bitstream processor, with virtual-address support for the signal processor and special pixel handling such as a Y/C split; imaging operations (pixel transfer scale and bias, convolution, minmax, histogram, colour matrix, look-up tables) in 16-bit fixed point; "3 Gops". Motion JPEG at up to 768 x 576 at 60 or 50 fields a second. MPEG is not in the chip (white paper, source 3).
- **Display engine (GBE, sections 1.5 and 9).** The DAC dot clock is 140 MHz for resolutions up to 1280 x 1024 at 76 Hz. Pixels are 8, 16 or 32 bits deep, in tiles of 128 lines by 512 bytes aligned on 64 KB, so 512 x 128, 256 x 128 or 128 x 128 pixels; a 1280 x 1024 x 32 frame buffer is exactly 10 x 8 tiles; normal planes are addressed by a tile pointer table. The overlay is 8 bits of colour index, always indexing the last 256-entry window of the 4608-entry colour map and always passing through the gamma map, with 0x00 transparent. The colour map is three 4608 x 8 maps indexed together for the 8- and 12-bit index modes and per component for RGB; the gamma map is three 256 x 8 maps; cursor pixels bypass the gamma map. **Established (published): RG3_B2, RGB4 and RGB5 pixels are expanded to RGB8 by bit replication before the colour map**, which [gbe.md](gbe.md) does not state. Screen capture redirects an NTSC- or PAL-sized region of the final image (after colour map, overlay, cursor and gamma) to memory, one field per retrace, by point-sampling every other line or by interpolating with "a 1/2, 1/4, or 1/9 flicker filter" (as printed; probably a misprint, the report gives no coefficients), and does not resample horizontally. The 4608-entry map and the capture path agree with [gbe.md](gbe.md).

Open questions moved: the dither format list; the 6-bit fraction (to established, published); the expansion rule for the narrow colour-index-free formats in the display path. Adds routes to blend, filter and antialiasing entries.

- Identity: SGI, "Unified Memory Architecture", white paper, 6 pages. https://www.sgidepot.co.uk/o2/1352.pdf (SHA-256 `984d35ab1ae7c3d74823bc90776d71541d177ce42f76eef13c35d750964ed395`).

## 3. O2 Digital Media white paper

- Identity: SGI, "Silicon Graphics O2 Workstation Digital Media White Paper". https://www.sgidepot.co.uk/o2/o2dm.pdf (SHA-256 `53aeaa0f9578b9dea07189b973fc2c97363a15ddbf6b483bad1405eac523d18a`).
- Stored copy: [docs/sources/reports/o2-digital-media-whitepaper.pdf](sources/reports/o2-digital-media-whitepaper.pdf), SHA-256 `53aeaa0f9578b9dea07189b973fc2c97363a15ddbf6b483bad1405eac523d18a`, library path `graphics/o2/o2-digital-media-whitepaper.pdf`.
- Covers: imaging, video and compression at product level. Match: exact.

Facts (established, published): "the O2 graphics engine processes data in 8-bit per component RGBA colour space" while video is 4:2:2 YCrCb of 8 or 10 bits, so the video path converts on entry; ICE does JPEG compression and decompression of video streams and texture images and accelerates the OpenGL imaging extensions; MPEG-1, H.261, Cinepak and Indeo are software. The 8-bit-per-component statement settles the blend operand width: the destination colour the blender reads is 8 bits per component, which [pixel-arithmetic.md](pixel-arithmetic.md) already derived from the frame formats. ## 4. OpenGL implementations paper (Graphics Hardware 1997)

- Identity: "Realizing OpenGL: Two Implementations of One Architecture", Graphics Hardware workshop, 1997, 11 pages. Copy read: the proceedings PDF (SHA-256 `c568a0eaabffc399c548c18f9a768a4d3156def7a6ab548cbae1cccdfffa26c3`). Its second half is the O2.
- Stored copy: [docs/sources/papers/kilgard97-realizing-opengl.pdf](sources/papers/kilgard97-realizing-opengl.pdf), SHA-256 `c568a0eaabffc399c548c18f9a768a4d3156def7a6ab548cbae1cccdfffa26c3`, library path `graphics/onyx/kilgard97-realizing-opengl.pdf`.
- Match: exact.

Facts (established, published):

- **Division of work (section 4).** The CPU does vertex processing (lighting, transformation, face culling, clipping), "calculates primitive plane equation parameters" and manages state; the ICE does most pixel-path transformations; the rendering engine does rasterisation, texture mapping and pixel update. The CPU sends commands to the rendering engine "using direct register writes". The O2 library has substantial CPU code and the common rendering paths are written in tuned MIPS assembly (compare the assembly units of [libglcore.md](libglcore.md)).
- **The rendering engine's blocks.** Three: a pixel pipeline that does all rasterisation, texturing and per-fragment operations; a memory request unit that queues colour, depth and stencil, clip-id and texture fetches; the memory transfer engine for fast clears and copies. Rasteriser stepping commands feed four parallel pipelines: colour, texture, stencil-and-depth, and address. The colour pipeline calculates fragment colour and applies the texture; the texture pipeline fetches texels and filters them to one texel; the stencil-and-depth pipeline discards fragments; the address pipeline clips fragments and forms the write address. Stippling, alpha test, window clipping and scissoring can fail a fragment, and a failed fragment updates neither colour nor depth. **Bearing:** this is the order of operations for the blend and depth entries, and it is first-party: depth and stencil tests discard before the colour write, and the colour pipeline fetches the destination colour for blending.
- **Fog.** The colour pipeline computes the fog blend factor while the texture pipeline waits for texels, because fog follows texture in OpenGL order and needs no memory read; so the fog factor is formed from interpolated values in the colour pipeline, not from the filtered texel. This agrees with source 1's "fog values linearly interpolated".
- **Rendering TLB (section 4.4).** All rendering memory accesses go through a rendering translation buffer in the memory request unit; entries must point to physical memory (no page faults); it is distinct from the CPU TLB. Each entry points to a 64 KB tile; a colour tile is 128 x 128 pixels of 32-bit RGBA; other tile types hold overlay, stencil and depth, texture and window-clip state; the display engine has the highest priority and scan-out uses about half the 2.1 GB/s. Tiles for a second colour buffer and for depth and stencil are allocated only where a window needs them.
- **Speed.** Over 60 million pixels a second; over 30 million with linear mipmap linear texturing; a 180 MHz R5000 sets up over 450,000 smooth-shaded, depth-tested, untextured 50-pixel triangles a second.

Open questions moved: none closed.

## 5. US 6,104,417, unified memory with dynamic graphics memory allocation

- Identity: US 6,104,417, "Unified memory computer architecture with dynamic graphics memory allocation", filed 1996-09-13, granted 2000-08-15, assigned to SGI by its inventors (a continuation application is US 2001/0019331; European family EP 0 829 820). https://patents.google.com/patent/US6104417A/en
- Stored copy: [docs/sources/patents/US6104417.pdf](sources/patents/US6104417.pdf), SHA-256 `4765de14ef12d18e9e4774060db4281f0e64932f7bc381f4bcaf32d002f6d874`, library path `patents/US6104417-o2-unified-memory-dynamic-graphics.pdf`.
- Covers: a memory controller and rendering engine on one IC (the text's "graphics rendering and memory controller IC"), a graphics back end, an I/O IC and an image processor and compression device (which a table names VICE, with MACE and GBE for the other clients), all sharing one SDRAM memory. The text does not use the product names; the match to CRIME, MACE, GBE and VICE rests on the table's labels, the 66 MHz clock and the 64 KB tile, and is high but **inferred**.
- Match: high. Facts **inferred**.

Facts:

- **Rendering engine.** A 2D and 3D coprocessor at 66 MHz, synchronous with the memory; four units: host interface (the programming-interface registers), pixel pipeline, memory transfer engine, memory request unit. The memory transfer engine does "byte-aligned clears and copies at memory bandwidth" on both linear buffers and frame buffers. The memory request unit arbitrates the pixel pipeline's requests and queues them to the memory controller. This agrees with source 4 and with the transfer engine's operations in [mte.md](mte.md).
- **Translation.** A frame-buffer translation buffer maps (x, y) to physical addresses; the CPU loads it with tile base addresses for a colour buffer and a stencil-depth buffer, with room for a 2048 x 2048 buffer of each, so 256 entries each ((2048/128)^2); a 64 KB tile holds 128 x 128 pixels at 32 bits, 256 x 128 at 16 bits or 512 x 128 at 8 bits; pixel buffers are n x n tiles with n = 4 preferred. A request through an invalid entry sets bit 6 of the 7-bit message the engine sends, and the controller then deasserts all external bank selects, so the access does not happen. **Where it meets the shipped chip:** [CRIME_RE.h](../CRIME_RE.h) has three frame-buffer translation groups of 64 registers with four 16-bit tile entries each (256 entries per group) and entries holding the physical address shifted right 16 bits (a 64 KB tile number). The 256 and the 64 KB agree; the patent has two buffers where the chip has three groups, so the third group is an extension or a different use (**unknown**). The message-bit-6 rule has no counterpart read in the driver; route: the status register's translation-miss bits in [spec] against the register groups.
- **Client interface.** Per request: a 3-bit command (1 read, 2 write, 4 read-modify-write), a 25-bit address, a 7-bit message (used by the rendering engine and the I/O IC to say which queue or subsystem is meant), 256 data bits each way and a 32-bit byte mask in which 0 means write the byte. The controller's data path is 288 lines at 66 MHz behind a 144-line, 133 MHz demultiplexer; SDRAM CAS latency 2, burst length 1, four open pages, eight DIMM slots, 32 MB to 1 GB, 4096 refreshes per 64 ms, single-bit ECC correction and multi-bit error interrupt.
- **Arbitration.** A slot scheme: graphics back end 1 slot in 2, I/O 1 in 4, image processor and compression 1 in 8, rendering engine 1 in 16, CPU 1 in 32, refresh 1 in 64; one round of 1062 cycles (15.93 us); the rendering engine may use up to eight memory words without a page crossing, or one read-modify-write, per slot, at most 12 cycles. The slot shares give I/O 256 MB/s, VICE 128 MB/s, the rendering engine 64 MB/s and the CPU 32 MB/s. The shipped statement (source 1) is a fixed priority order, display, audio and video, ICE, rendering engine, CPU, which has the same ranking but no slot weights, so the slot scheme is the design the patent describes and is **not** claimed for the shipped chip. [spec] was not rechecked for arbitration in this pass.
- Not in the patent: pixel-pipeline widths, interpolation, blending, texturing, dithering, register maps, FIFO depths. The FIFO-depth conflict (128 in the specification, 64 in NetBSD) is untouched.

## 7. US 5,490,240, three-dimensional textures

- Identity: US 5,490,240, "System and method of generating interactive computer graphic images incorporating three dimensional textures", filed 1993-07-09, granted 1996-02-06, assigned to SGI. https://patents.google.com/patent/US5490240A/en
- Stored copy: [docs/sources/patents/US5490240.pdf](sources/patents/US5490240.pdf), SHA-256 `5a458b3d356fdde064a1cd1a323a0b8aa1ec17be2f8986df59073bad199fa3cf`, library path `patents/US5490240-three-dimensional-textures.pdf`.
- Match: lineage only. The page names no product.
- Facts (**inferred** for CRIME): the level of detail of a 2 x 2 quad (or per pixel) is the maximum of absolute differences of the texture coordinates across a step in x and in y; the fine level is `max(0, int(log2(LOD)))`, the coarse level is fine + 1, and the level fraction is `(LOD >> fine) - 1.0`, that is the LOD divided by 2^fine minus one, linear in LOD, not in log2 LOD; eight neighbouring texels are addressed from the integer parts, a trilinear lookup reads 16, and two 8-way interpolations are blended by the level fraction. **Bearing:** this is the candidate for the two entries in [pixel-arithmetic.md](pixel-arithmetic.md) that are "unknown: no shipped software or published source depends on it", the precision of the level of detail and how its fraction weights the two levels. The specification takes `LOD = log2(rho)` with rho a maximum; the patent's maximum matches. The linear fraction is a design of the generation before; whether CRIME keeps it is unknown. A discriminating reading: the diagnostic's texture tests, if any lists a level fraction (the O2 diagnostics were not read for this).

## 8. US 5,528,738 and US 5,581,680, line antialiasing

- Stored copy: [docs/sources/patents/US5528738.pdf](sources/patents/US5528738.pdf), SHA-256 `cb47d3ef58cce10ba21f6dff92fad75aaa5f2e227efd5dc26441acfc71774145`, library path `patents/US5528738-antialiasing-polygonal-images.pdf`; [docs/sources/patents/US5581680.pdf](sources/patents/US5581680.pdf), SHA-256 `e5e877440c75a70d7867cf9e8f76df553548703e5123d59aabbf2b51056b4c26`, library path `patents/US5581680-antialiasing-raster-images.pdf`.
- Covers: an extension of Bresenham's algorithm that writes two pixels per step with coverage from a table, for a desktop workstation (the text names the Indy). Match: lineage only.
- Facts (**inferred** for CRIME's antialiased lines): coordinates 16.4, deltas 17.4, the decision value and a sign term 18.8; the vertical distance to the pixel centre carried with 4 fraction bits and an accumulated error under 2^-5 over a 2^16 range; coverage from eight tables, one per octant, of 4-bit entries (16 entries to a 64-bit table), addressed by the distance and its one's complement together to give both pixels' coverage; a circular cone filter; endpoint coverage the table entry scaled by the end point's fractional coordinate (or its complement at the start point); for fractional end points a corrected first decision value; two pixels per iteration; blending `alpha x colour + (1 - alpha) x buffer`. **Bearing:** the O2 report's "two-pixel-wide lines", "slope- and distance-based coverage tables in ROM" and "filtered line endpoints" read like this design, and the specification's 2.14 slope field ([context.md](context.md)) plays the part of the patent's slope term. The widths differ (the O2 has 6 fraction bits, the patent 4), so the patent is not the CRIME circuit. It names the design's ingredients and nothing else: the table contents and widths of the O2 stay unknown. Route: the specification's antialiased-line step, with the patent's decision terms as the vocabulary to look for.

## 9. Hardware Developer's Handbook, release 2.0

- Identity: SGI, "Hardware Developers Handbook", release 2.0, May 1997, 339 PDF pages. Stored copy: [docs/sources/manuals/hardware-developers-handbook-2.0.pdf](sources/manuals/hardware-developers-handbook-2.0.pdf), SHA-256 `3da04c67414ac5794a1f04cb4a207e68919db25d10f518c9b599adb7a3805d8d`, library path `sgi-hw/hardware-developer-handbook-2.0.pdf`. Page numbers below are PDF pages.
- Covers: bus, connector and board facts for every SGI desktop and server of the day; the O2 appears in the system table, the chassis chapter, the memory, monitor, video and PCI sections. It has no register information.
- Match: exact, product level.

Facts (established, published):

- **Names.** The system table lists the O2 as development name "Moosehead" with graphics "CRM" and CPU board "IP32" (PDF 29). This agrees with the board name used throughout this workspace and with [mace-video.md](mace-video.md), which quotes the same development name from the MACE specification.
- **Monitor connector.** The O2 uses a 15-pin D-sub monitor connector. Pin 9 carries +5 V limited to 0.5 A on a fuse shared with the mouse and keyboard, and pins 12 and 15 carry the two I2C lines to the monitor, with pin 11 as the older monitor-type pin (PDF 167, table 4-36 and its notes). The 007-3275 table (source 10) gives the same assignment, so the monitor I2C wiring is established by two published sources, and the `I2C` register of [gbe.md](gbe.md) is the host end of those two pins (inferred, because neither document names GBE).
- **Video connectors.** On the O2 the analog S-video output is a four-pin mini-DIN and the input a seven-pin mini-DIN whose extra pins carry I2C data, clock and 12 V (PDF 185). The digital video connector is one 68-pin connector with one input port and one output port (PDF 190), and the protocol is close to but not the same as parallel CCIR 601. These match the two D1 ports and analog encoder of [mace-video.md](mace-video.md).
- **Memory and expansion.** Memory modules are 278-pin DIMMs of 32 or 64 MB with ECC bits (PDF 159); there is one half-size PCI slot, 5 V only, with less power than the other machines (PDF 220, 232). The 278-pin count and the 288-bit width of source 11 fit one 256-bit word plus 32 check bits; inferred.

## What these sources settle, by open question

- **Rendering engine arithmetic, vertex position:** the 6 fraction bits are established (published).
- **Dither:** which formats (3:3:2 and 5:5:5), not the matrix.
- **Blend arithmetic (product width, rounding, saturation):** not moved; the operands are 8-bit (source 3). Blend follows the colour pipeline's destination read; depth and stencil discard before it (source 4).
- **Texel filter weights and level-of-detail fraction:** a candidate fraction formula from a predecessor (source 7, inferred); weights still unknown.
- **Memory transfer engine and tiling:** 64 KB tiles, 256-entry translation per buffer, clears and copies at memory bandwidth, and a blend-capable pixel-transfer path (sources 1, 4 and 5).
- **Antialiased lines:** two-pixel-wide, table-driven, coverage to alpha (source 1); design vocabulary from source 8.
- **Shipped CRIME revision, FIFO depth and interrupt numbering:** not reached by any published source.
- **ICE and VICE:** the register map, DMA, translation and host interface are now read from the VICE specification ([vice.md](vice.md)); the signal-processor and bitstream instruction sets are still unread (the specification copy lacks its appendix).
